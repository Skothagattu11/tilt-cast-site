A phone held still is dead on screen.

Dead‑still reads as a static mockup, which is why real product films always keep a slow drift going. TiltCast layers procedural motion on top of your live device motion, built from sines at incommensurate frequencies so the loop never visibly repeats.

It ships inside your app. So you should be able to read it.

There is nothing here worth licensing. It is about 200 lines of CoreMotion and a socket, reimplementable from the wire format in an afternoon, so gating it would cost adoption and protect nothing.

The SDK is MIT‑licensed and public. The paid product is the Mac app that renders and records; this just makes your phone reachable. It is wrapped in #if DEBUG, so it compiles out of your App Store binary entirely.

The constraints, up front.

Can I film an app I did not build?

No. iOS has no CoreMotion background mode, so device motion stops the moment your app leaves the foreground. A separate companion app could only ever film itself, which means the SDK has to live inside the app you are filming. If you need to record someone else's app, record it flat and add procedural camera motion in post.

Why does it need a cable?

Because macOS only exposes a cabled iPhone's screen as a capture device. A Wi‑Fi‑paired phone shows as connected but produces no capture device at all. That is the platform, not a choice we made. If you see nothing, check the cable first.

An SDK, in my app?

In your debug build only. The call sits behind #if DEBUG, so it and the SDK's behaviour compile out of your App Store binary entirely. It is MIT‑licensed and about 200 lines, so you can read every line before you agree to ship it.

Does it phone home?

The SDK makes zero outbound network connections. It binds a local listener and waits; the Mac connects in over usbmux, through the cable. No analytics, no telemetry, no servers, and no Local Network permission prompt for your users.

Why isn't this on the Mac App Store?

The App Sandbox blocks the USB device path TiltCast uses to reach the phone. It is distributed directly instead, signed with a Developer ID certificate and notarized by Apple, so it opens cleanly on your Mac.

What happens if I cancel?

You drop to the Free tier and keep every project and recording you have made. Exports become watermarked again. A lifetime purchase never lapses.

What do I need?

macOS 14 or later, an iPhone and a cable, and an Xcode project you can compile. The SDK requires iOS 16 or later. There is no Android or Windows path.
